# Block F: adversarial security evaluation

**Run:** 2026-11-19, 04:03–05:15 UTC. Branch `akhil`, uncommitted. SUMO 1.27.1, Python 3.11.4. **Simulation only:** synthetic 5x5 urban grid, synthetic demand, abstracted watchdog. The VeReMi ML model is not used anywhere in this block.

**Sources.** All numbers come from `results/adversarial/`:
- per-run CSVs `<sweep>_{genuine,trust,forgery}_runs.csv`;
- aggregated `<sweep>_*_summary.csv`;
- every table at every density in [`results/adversarial/BLOCK_F_TABLES.md`](results/adversarial/BLOCK_F_TABLES.md), regenerated by `python -X utf8 results/adversarial/analysis/blockf_tables.py`.

**Intervals.** Unless marked otherwise, a cell is mean ± 95 % CI half-width over 30 independent runs: Student t with df = n − 1, the same `metrics.summarize` as the published results. The tables below show 200 vehicles; 100 and 300 are in `BLOCK_F_TABLES.md`.

## 1. Summary

1. **Relay dropping does not hurt broadcast delivery in these networks.**
   - With up to 30 % attackers dropping every alert they receive, the proposed scheme reached 100 % of reachable honest vehicles in 99.98 % of 4,494 accepted genuine runs (minimum 97.8 %).
   - Flooding averaged 99.6 % but fell below 90 % in 4 seeds. One of them was a broadcast storm; in two others the shortfall appeared only when attackers dropped every copy (section 8).
   - The single-path CH route is the exception. Its relayed RSU reception falls when attackers are trusted (on-off), but those cells hold only 8–15 runs each, so the drop is inconclusive.
2. **The trust gate stops persistent and half-time attackers, not mostly-cooperative ones.**
   - At gate 0.70, 100 % of attackers are below the gate when they always fail the watchdog (cooperation 0), cooperate half the time (0.5), or have a phased duty of 0.5.
   - At cooperation 0.9 or duty ≥ 0.9, only 0–1.7 % are. AUC falls to 0.81 at duty 0.95.
   - No honest vehicle falls below 0.70 in any run.
3. **On-off attackers defeat the default forgery protection.** With attackers cooperating 90 % of the time, the full default level (voting, BLS and reputation) accepts the forged alert in 83–100 % of runs at 200 vehicles. Over all of sweeps B and C at the 0.70 gate it accepted 1,200 of 3,060 forgeries.
4. **The opt-in committee check closes most of the gap.**
   - Counting only committee endorsers cuts accepted forgeries to 47 of 3,060.
   - It is 0 % at 10 % attackers and ≤ 3.3 % at 20 %, but 26.7 % at 30 % attackers with cooperation 0.9 (200 vehicles), when colluders hold 3 of the 5 committee seats.
   - It never rejected a genuine alert: 100 % of accepted genuine certificates pass the check.
5. **Raising the gate also works, but excludes honest vehicles.** Calibration on held-out seeds chose 0.88. On the evaluation seeds it accepts 0 % of forgeries, but 34–38 % of honest vehicles fall below it (lose reputable status). The committee check at 0.70 gives a similar forgery rate with 0 % false alarms.
6. **Vote withholding is the main threat to genuine alerts.**
   - In every one of 6,120 runs, the genuine alert failed exactly when 3 or more attackers sat on its 5-member committee: 189 failures, 0 exceptions.
   - With cooperation 0.9 this cost up to 23 % of genuine alerts at 30 % attackers.
   - No tested defence prevents it.
7. **The colluder limit (3 versus 5) changes nothing measurable.** The source releases the certificate on the third vote, and colluders vote in rank order. In 3,239 of 3,240 run pairs the outcome is identical; only the vote traffic differs.

**Dropping behaves as configured:**
- Dropped copies equal the expected share × drop probability × receivers; for example, 60.0 ± 0.0 for 30 % attackers at drop probability 1.
- Drop probability never changes whether the genuine alert is accepted (0 of 540 run groups differ), because dropping affects relaying, not voting.

**What it does to delivery:**
- **Broadcast schemes:** delivery stays at about 100 %. Dense grids give every honest vehicle several paths.
- **CH route:** the single path is where dropping matters. Relayed RSU reception at 100 vehicles falls from 83.3 % (always-dropping attackers, 30 %) to 33.3 % (on-off attackers, 30 %), because trusted attackers become route heads.
- **Caveat:** only 8–15 incidents per cell need a relay, so these CIs span ±19 to ±38 points. Treat the CH-route effect as indicative, not established.

**Reading the AUC.** AUC above 0.93 still means low trust ranks attackers below honest vehicles. The problem is that their trust sits above the 0.70 gate. The Beta trust approaches the share of watchdog observations a vehicle passes:
- about 0.9 × 0.9 = 0.81 for an attacker that cooperates 90 % of the time and is also missed 10 % of the time like everyone else;
- about 0.90 for an honest vehicle.

At duty 0.95 the two distributions overlap and ranking also degrades (AUC 0.73–0.83). The phase period (10 s against 30 s) changes AUC only at duty 0.95. Results at 20 % attackers are the same within the CIs (`BLOCK_F_TABLES.md`).

## 7. Collusion (D)

- **Colluding signers on forged alerts:** see section 5. The committee check is what limits them; the colluder limit is not.
- **Colluder limit 3 against 5:**
  - Same outcome in 3,239 of 3,240 run pairs (B, all settings and densities). The one difference (d200_r08, 30 % attackers, cooperation 0.9) is a delivery share of 98.6 % against 100 %; both accepted.
  - Transmissions differ in 1,957 pairs, from the extra colluder votes.
  - Reason: the source releases the certificate on the QUORUM-th (3rd) vote, and colluders vote in rank order, so colluders 4 and 5 never enter a certificate.
- **Vote withholding on genuine alerts:** attackers on the committee stay silent.
  - Over all 6,120 runs, the genuine alert was accepted in all 5,931 runs with at most 2 attackers on the committee. It failed in all 189 runs with 3 or more.
  - The gate keeps always-dropping and half-time attackers off committees (0.0 attackers per committee), so their genuine acceptance is 100 %.
  - With cooperation 0.9 or duty ≥ 0.9, genuine acceptance at 200 vehicles is 100 % (10 % attackers), 86.7 ± 12.9 % (20 %) and 83.3 ± 14.2 % (30 %). At 100 vehicles with 30 % it is 76.7 ± 16.1 %.
  - Neither the committee check nor gate 0.88 is designed against this. Gate 0.88 removes most attackers from committees but also 36 % of honest vehicles.
- **Reputation manipulation (bad-mouthing, ballot-stuffing):** not applicable. Reputation is one global watchdog value with no recommendations to manipulate (audit, Block F phase 1).

## 8. Genuine-alert delivery and acceptance

- **Acceptance:** 100 % in every setting where attackers stay below the gate. Lower only through vote withholding (section 7).
- **Delivery once accepted:** the proposed scheme reaches every reachable honest vehicle in 99.98 % of runs (4,494 runs across A, B and C; minimum 97.8 %).
- **Flooding** averages 99.6 % (sweep A) but fell below 90 % in 4 distinct seeds:
  - d200_r00 at 55.9 %, a broadcast storm with a 92 % collision ratio; the proposed scheme reached all 179 honest vehicles in the same run;
  - d100_r27 at 78.7 %;
  - d300_r18 at 87.0 % and d300_r29 at 90.0 %. These two fell short only when attackers drop every copy (drop probability 1, 20–30 % attackers), so dropping contributes there.
- **Committee-check false rejections:** none (section 5).
- **Time to quorum:** 12.0–12.1 ms in every accepted genuine run (7 distinct values, set by the committee's vote slots). It is unaffected by the attacks, because silent members are skipped, not waited for.
- **CH route:** relayed RSU reception at 33–100 %, with too few runs per cell to rank settings (section 3).

## 9. Failed, incomplete or inconclusive

- **Failed or incomplete:** none. 68 of 68 settings and 6,120 of 6,120 runs completed. No run raised an error. The RSU's "a genuine alert failed RSU validation" assertion never fired.
- **Inconclusive:**
  1. CH-route relayed RSU reception: only 8–15 incidents per cell need a relay. All its differences, including attack effects and gate 0.88, lie within overlapping 95 % CIs.
  2. Colluder limit 3 against 5: no effect, by protocol design (section 7). This is not a statement about larger collusions under a different release rule.
  3. Phase period (10 s against 30 s): differs only in AUC at duty 0.95 (0.72–0.74 against 0.80–0.83). Forgery and delivery results agree within CIs.
  4. The calibrated gate 0.88 rests on a rule without a false-alarm limit (section 4). A rule with such a limit would choose differently or find no gate. That is a design decision, not something the data settles.
- **Single runs worth noting:**
  - the four flooding runs below 90 % delivery (section 8);
  - the 3-against-5 colluder difference (d200_r08, explained above).

## 10. Limitations (state these with any result)

1. **Simulation only:** one synthetic grid and synthetic demand at 100–300 vehicles. Attackers are a fixed random 10–30 % of vehicles.
2. **Abstracted watchdog.** Each second, each observed vehicle passes or fails a coin flip, with honest relays missed 10 % of the time. Routine relaying is not simulated as packets. Trust is one global value per vehicle and does not fade.
3. **One alert per run.** Dropping an alert never lowers the dropper's trust in a later event; the post-event trust update is logged but never used.
4. **Attacker behaviour on the alert.** Attackers always drop the alert (subject to the drop probability) regardless of their on-off phase. They always withhold genuine votes and always endorse their own side's forgeries.
5. **Committee check assumptions.** It assumes receivers can rebuild the committee of the claimed incident from beacon positions and network-wide reputation. Here the positions come from the recorded snapshot; position spoofing is not modelled.
6. **Keys.** They are derived from vehicle IDs, so there are no Sybil identities. The forger can only claim its own position.
7. **Measurement caveats.**
   - Detection and false-alarm rates are measured at the gate in use.
   - AUC is computed per run over all vehicles, including vehicles with few watchdog observations (trust near the 0.5 prior).
   - CIs treat runs as independent. Within one setting they are; across settings the same seeds recur, so settings are paired, not independent.
