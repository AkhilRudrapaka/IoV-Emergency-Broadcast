1Overview

Goal

When a vehicle crashes, the vehicles around it should learn of it within tens of milliseconds, and a roadside unit (RSU) should register the event for the emergency services. Two risks stand in the way: on a shared radio channel, many vehicles rebroadcasting the same alert collide (the broadcast storm), and a misbehaving vehicle could report an accident that never happened.

What this demo shows

One comparison pair = one incident scenario (fixed network, traffic, incident and seed). The pair is replayed twice in SUMO-GUI, one approach per run: first a baseline only, then the proposed method only. Every scheme of a pair runs on the same simulated snapshot, so the comparison below is like-for-like.

The proposed method, in plain words

Corroborate, then forward selectively. The crashed vehicle asks its nearest reputable neighbours to vote; the alert is released only with 3 of 5 votes, whose signatures are combined into one compact certificate that every receiver checks. Cluster heads and trusted vehicles relay first, farther ones before nearer ones, and a vehicle stays silent when all its neighbours already have the alert. As one stage of the same method, a CH-to-CH route carries the alert along trusted cluster heads to the nearest RSU.

The baselines

Four forwarding baselines are implemented, all with the same voting and certificate check and the same radio: blind flooding (every vehicle rebroadcasts once), flooding + random delay, slotted 1-persistence (farther receivers rebroadcast earlier; any duplicate suppresses) and slotted 1-p + random delay. They differ from the proposed method only in how the alert is forwarded.

    6Evidence & limitations

      Limitations

      • The radio is a simulated IEEE 802.11p model (unit disk, 300 m) without fading or capture; the incident is staged (no crash detection).
      • SUMO-GUI replays a run that the experiment engine computed on the identical SUMO state; the demo decides nothing itself, and every replay ends on the record's own totals.
      • A pair is one run of one scenario: it illustrates the behaviour and supports no statistical claim. The multi-seed results above are the evidence for general statements.
      • The CH-to-CH route is a stage of the proposed method, not a separate algorithm. The baselines share the proposed method's voting and certificate check and differ only in forwarding.
      • Scenarios are the experiment's 30 seeds at 200 vehicles on one 5 × 5 urban grid; once every location has been used, a reused one is labelled as a repeat.
      • Dispatch is a local outbox only; no emergency service is contacted. Runs cannot be paused or resumed.
